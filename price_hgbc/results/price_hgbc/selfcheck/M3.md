## 3. Permutation importance on 2014 (`src/price_hgbc/m3_permutation.py`; no refit)

Scorer: `pred = predict_proba[:,1] >= 0.45`, scored with `da.balanced_accuracy`. n_repeats 10, random_state 0;
importance = baseline − permuted (pp).

| Group | Group permutation (pp) | Ablation Δ (pp) |
|---|---|---|
| threshold_gap | **14.055** ± 0.354 | −2.721 (CI excludes 0) |
| weekday | 3.484 ± 0.087 | −0.389 (CI includes 0) |
| slot | 2.253 ± 0.120 | −0.606 (CI excludes 0) |
| demand_lag | 1.481 ± 0.217 | +0.216 (CI includes 0) |
| price_lag | 0.834 ± 0.123 | −0.382 (CI includes 0) |
| season | 0.412 ± 0.084 | +0.004 (CI includes 0) |

Per column, top 3: `lag1_price_gap` 11.770, `nsw_demand_lag7` 2.310, `weekday_0` 1.461.
One-hot, sin/cos and linearly dependent columns are reported only, not read on their own (grey in the figure).

**Agreement / disagreement.** Both methods rank threshold & gap first, and both detect slot. Weekday, demand lags and
price lags reduce BalAcc when permuted but not detectably when removed and refitted. Likely reasons (not forced into agreement):
* permutation measures this fitted model's reliance, while ablation measures what a refit can recover from other columns;
* redundancy: `nsw_demand_lag7` comes from the same weekday last week and carries a weekday signal;
* permutation creates combinations never seen in training (e.g. `weekday_0 = 1` with `weekend = 1`);
* the ablation CIs are about ±0.5–1 pp wide, so smaller contributions are not detectable.
